What to Prepare Before Your First Discovery Call

By the Vantage Studio team 6 min read

Quick answer

Bring three things: a plain-language description of what the site needs to do, two or three examples of sites you like (and specifically why), and a realistic sense of timeline and budget range. That's enough for a genuinely useful discovery conversation — you don't need a finished brief or a technical spec to get started.

Why discovery shapes everything downstream

Discovery is the research phase that happens before any design work starts — understanding the problem, not yet solving it. UX researchers at Nielsen Norman Group define it precisely this way: a preliminary phase focused on framing the problem and gathering enough evidence to know what to do next, deliberately separate from testing solutions. Skipping it doesn't save time. It just moves the cost of getting something wrong to a later, more expensive stage — a redesign after launch instead of a correction before it.

The three things worth preparing

You don't need a polished document. Three things genuinely move a discovery conversation forward:

  • A plain-language description of the problem. Not a feature list — what's actually not working today, or what the business needs the new site to do that it can't do now.
  • Two or three reference sites, and why. "I like this one" is less useful than "I like how this one handles their pricing page" — specificity here saves real back-and-forth later.
  • A realistic timeline and budget range. Not an exact number — just whether you're thinking in weeks or months, and roughly what range you're planning around.

Questions to expect on the call

Expect to be asked what success looks like six months after launch, who the site's actual visitors are and what they're trying to do, what's worked and what hasn't on the current site (if there is one), and who the final decision-maker is on design choices. None of these need a rehearsed answer — the point of the call is to think through them together, not to test whether you already know them.

What happens after the call

You get a written proposal — scope, timeline, and cost — usually within a few business days. If you move forward, the project follows our fixed five-stage process from there, so discovery isn't a one-off conversation disconnected from the rest of the build; it's the first of five stages that all connect.

How Vantage runs discovery

Discovery at Vantage is a real conversation, not a form to fill out — covered under the strategy side of our services, alongside design and development rather than treated as a separate add-on. It's run by the same in-house team you'll work with through launch, which you can read more about on our about page — no handoff between whoever sells the project and whoever builds it. If you want a sense of what comes out the other side, our recent work is a fair sample across industries.

Logic

A discovery call is only as useful as the specificity brought into it — vague input produces a vague proposal, regardless of how good the studio is.
